# Read a Bazi chart alongside its sources

These seven worked lessons use 17 birth inputs from the OpenFate Bazi Corpus. Each example uses UTC+8 at 120°E, with true solar time enabled. The inputs represent calendar slots. The work and relationship passages are editorial scenarios. You can read the lessons on GitHub or inspect the exact records offline. [繁體中文課程](README.md) · [Evidence file](evidence.json)

## 1. Find a Ten God that never appears in the top row

On January 1, 1900 at 00:30, the four pillars are **己亥 · 丙子 · 甲戌 · 甲子**. Read the first character of the third pillar, 甲, as the Day Master. 辛 metal controls 甲 wood and has the opposite polarity, making it Direct Officer (正官). There is no 辛 on the top row. Look inside the day branch 戌: its hidden stems are 戊, 辛 and 丁. At 02:30 the hour becomes 乙丑. Another 辛 appears inside 丑. Both occurrences are hidden. [Chart table and classical example](reading-pillars.md)

## 2. Understand why different hour stems produce the same paragraph

For October 18, 1991, the hour stems at 08:30, 10:30 and 12:30 are 壬, 癸 and 甲. The published salary discussion groups the first two as visible output stars, 傷官 and 食神. In both cases it suggests taking a piece of delivered work to the meeting. The third case discusses evidence of prevented errors. By contrast, the autumn 辛 chapter of *Qiong Tong Bao Jian* names 壬 and 甲 separately. A rule for selecting contemporary prose can group stems that matter individually to a classical method. [Classical pair and published rules](schools.md)

## 3. Tell a matching hidden stem from a strength judgment

On February 29, 2000 the Day Master is 丁. At 10:30 there is no hidden 丁, although several branches contain 丙, another fire stem. At 12:30 the hour branch 午 contains 丁 as its principal stem. At 14:30 the branch 未 contains 丁 in a secondary position. The binary question “Is 丁 present?” gives the same answer for the last two cases. The positions carry different weight in the source discussion. [Root table and scanned source](roots.md)

## 4. Name both ends of an interaction

On October 18, 1991 at 06:30, the hour branch 卯 combines with month branch 戌 and clashes with day branch 酉. At 08:30, hour branch 辰 clashes with the month and combines with the day. Both charts contain a combination and a clash. The day branch participates in different relationships. A further judgment about whether a combination resolves a clash requires its own rules and evidence. [Diagram and classical counterexample](interactions.md)

## 5. Read the onset receipt

The 08:30 chart on October 18, 1991 has the same natal pillars under both sex inputs used by the traditional direction rule. The female input counts forward to 立冬 and starts the 己亥 cycle on October 6, 1998. The male input counts back to 寒露 and starts 丁酉 on November 14, 1994. The boundary instants in the receipt use UTC. The recorded onset uses nominal local civil time. [Dates, direction rule and source](luck-direction.md)

## 6. Recognize a result that must remain open

On February 5, 1932 the engine’s adjacent records put 立春 at 08:29:20 UTC+8. For the 08:30 female input, the civil instant is forty seconds later. Its true solar clock reads 08:15:56. The engine records a pillar/term boundary mismatch. The published record retains its four pillars and natal readings, with an empty annual series. The adjacent 06:30 and 10:30 records have calculated onsets and different first cycles. [Three records and the calendar reference](solar-term.md)

## 7. Compare a year with the active decade

On July 1, 2026 the October 1991 native is 34. In both published direction variants the annual pillar is 丙午. The female input is in 辛丑, while the male input is in 甲午. In the latter, the annual and decade branches both read 午, but the stems differ. Calling them identical pillars would discard that distinction.
